### Question
**3] Increase in absolute and per capita real GNP do not connote a higher level of economic development,if: [2018]

**a) Industrial output fails to keep pace with agricultural output.
b) Agricultural output fails to keep pace with industrial output.
c) Poverty and unemployment increases.
d) Imports grow faster than exports.

**Answer:**
Ans: c) Poverty and unemployment increases.

**Explanation:**
- **Gross National Product (GNP)**&nbsp;represents the total market value of final goods and services produced in a country.



- **Real GNP**&nbsp;adjusts for inflation, providing a more accurate picture of economic growth.



- **Per capita real GNP**&nbsp;is real GNP divided by the population, indicating average economic output per person.




**An increase in these figures suggests economic growth, but it doesn’t necessarily translate to economic development.**





**Economic development** encompasses a broader notion of well-being beyond just economic output. It includes factors like:






- **Improved living standards:**&nbsp;This involves aspects like access to healthcare, education, and basic necessities.



- **Reduced poverty and unemployment:**&nbsp;A growing economy with widespread poverty and unemployment signifies unequal distribution of benefits, hindering development.



- **Infrastructure development:**&nbsp;Investments in roads, transportation, and communication networks improve overall economic efficiency and quality of life.
