### Question
**4] Despite being a high saving economy, capital formation may not result in significant increase in output due to: [2018]

**a) Weak administrative machinery
b) Illiteracy
c) High population density
d) High capital-output ratio

**Answer:**
Ans: d) High capital-output ratio

**Explanation:**
- **Capital formation**&nbsp;refers to the process of saving and investing money to create new capital goods (machinery, equipment, infrastructure).



- A high saving rate in an economy is generally considered positive as it provides funds for investment.




However, simply having a high saving rate doesn’t guarantee a significant increase in output (production). The impact of capital formation on output depends on the **capital-output ratio (COR)**.






- **Capital-output ratio** measures the amount of additional capital investment required to produce one unit of additional output.



A **high capital-output ratio** indicates that a large amount of capital investment is needed to generate a relatively small increase in output. This can happen due to various reasons:

- **Inefficient industries:**&nbsp;Existing industries might be outdated or inefficient, requiring significant investments just to maintain current output levels, not necessarily increase them.



- **Infrastructure bottlenecks:**&nbsp;Lack of proper infrastructure like transportation, power, or communication networks can hinder the utilization of new capital effectively.



- **Technological limitations:**&nbsp;If the economy lacks access to advanced technologies, even large investments might not translate into substantial output growth.
