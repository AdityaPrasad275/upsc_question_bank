### Question
**2] The problem of international liquidity is related to the non-availability of: [2015]

**a) Goods and services
b) Gold and silver
c) Dollars and other hard currencies
d) Exportable surplus

**Answer:**
Ans: c) Dollars and other hard currencies

**Explanation:**
- **International liquidity**&nbsp;refers to the ease with which a country can settle its international financial obligations. It essentially reflects how readily a country has access to foreign currencies needed for international transactions.



- **Goods and services (a):**&nbsp;While the availability of goods and services is important for international trade, it doesn’t directly address the issue of international liquidity, which focuses on currencies used for settlements.



- **Gold and silver (b):**&nbsp;While historically used as a form of international reserves, gold and silver are not as widely accepted for international transactions compared to major currencies like the US dollar.



- **Exportable surplus (d):**&nbsp;Having a surplus of exports over imports can be a source of foreign currency earnings, but it doesn’t guarantee sufficient international liquidity. A country might still face difficulty settling its obligations if the surplus currency is not widely accepted or readily convertible.




**Dollars and other hard currencies (c)** are the most critical elements for international liquidity. Countries need these currencies to:






- Pay for imports of goods and services



- Repay international debt



- Intervene in foreign exchange markets to stabilize their exchange rates
