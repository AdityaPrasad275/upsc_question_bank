### Question
**14] The growth rate of per capita income at current prices is higher than that of per capita income at constant prices, because the latter takes into account the rate of: [2000]**

a) Growth of population
b) Increase in price level
c) Growth of money supply
d) Increase in the wage rate

**Answer:**
Ans:  b) Increase in price level

**Explanation:**
- **Per capita income at current prices:** This refers to the total income of a country divided by its population, without any adjustment for inflation. So, it reflects the income in the current year’s prices.



- **Per capita income at constant prices:** This takes into account inflation. It calculates the income per person using the prices of a specific base year, allowing for a more accurate comparison of income levels across time.



- **Impact of inflation:** When the price level increases (inflation), the same amount of money can buy fewer goods and services. This means that per capita income at current prices might show an increase simply due to inflation, even if the real purchasing power of the income hasn’t necessarily improved.
