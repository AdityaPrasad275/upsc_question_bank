### Question 16
1] **Which of the following are the methods of Parliamentary control over public finance in India: [2012]**

1. Placing Annual Financial Statement before the Parliament
2. Withdrawal of moneys from Consolidated Fund of India only after passing the Appropriation Bill
3. Provisions of supplementary grants and vote-an-account
4. A periodic or at least a mid-year review of programme of the Government against macroeconomic forecasts and expenditure by a Parliamentary Budget Office
5. Introducing Finance Bill in the Parliament

Select the correct answer using the codes given below:

a) 1, 2, 3 &amp; 5 Only

b) 1, 2 &amp; 4 only

c) 3, 4 &amp; 5 Only

d) 1, 2, 3 , 4 &amp; 5 Only

**Answer:**
Ans: 
a) 1, 2, 3 &amp; 5 Only

**Explanation:**
- **Placing Annual Financial Statement before the Parliament**: This is indeed a method of Parliamentary control over public finance. The Annual Financial Statement, also known as the Union Budget, is presented to Parliament for approval.

- **Withdrawal of moneys from Consolidated Fund of India only after passing the Appropriation Bill**: This is a key aspect of Parliamentary control. No money can be withdrawn from the Consolidated Fund of India without the authorization of Parliament through the passing of the Appropriation Bill.

- **Provisions of supplementary grants and vote-on-account**: This is another method of Parliamentary control. Supplementary grants and vote-on-account are provided by Parliament to meet additional expenditure requirements or to allow for government spending in the interim period before the budget is passed.

- **A periodic or at least a mid-year review of the programme of the Government against macroeconomic forecasts and expenditure by a Parliamentary Budget Office**: As of 2012, there wasn’t a formal Parliamentary Budget Office in India. Therefore, this option may not be considered a method of Parliamentary control at that time.

- **Introducing Finance Bill in the Parliament**: The Finance Bill is introduced in Parliament to give effect to the financial proposals of the government for the upcoming fiscal year. This is a crucial aspect of Parliamentary control over public finance.
