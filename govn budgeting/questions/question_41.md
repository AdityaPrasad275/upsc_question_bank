Based on this table, it can be said that the Indian economy is in poor shape because the Central Government continues to be under pressure to


a) Reduce expenditure of defence

b) Spend more and more on interest payments

c) Reduce expenditure on subsidies

d) Spend more and more as grants-in-aid to State Governments/Union Territories

**Answer:**
Ans: 
b) Spend more and more on interest payments

**Explanation:**
- **Defence**: The percentage of expenditure on defence has decreased slightly from 15.1% in 1989-90 to 13.6% in 1994-95. While defence spending is significant, a slight decrease in its share of total expenditure may not necessarily indicate a poor economic state on its own.

- **Interest Payments**: The percentage allocated to interest payments has increased substantially from 27.7% in 1989-90 to 38.7% in 1994-95. This significant increase suggests a growing burden of debt service, which can strain government finances and indicate challenges in managing public debt.

- **Subsidies**: The percentage allocated to subsidies has decreased notably from 16.3% in 1989-90 to 8% in 1994-95. A reduction in subsidies could indicate efforts to rationalize government spending and improve fiscal discipline. However, it may also impact certain sectors or sections of society dependent on subsidies.

- **Grants to States/Uts**: The percentage allocated to grants to States/UTs has increased slightly from 13.6% in 1989-90 to 16.7% in 1994-95. This increase could reflect greater devolution of funds to state governments for various development projects and welfare programs.

- **Others**: The category of “Others” includes various expenditures not specifically mentioned. It has decreased slightly from 27.4% in 1989-90 to 23% in 1994-95.

Given this analysis:

- Option 
b) Spend more and more on interest payments seems to be the most concerning as it indicates a growing burden of debt service, which could strain government finances and hinder overall economic growth.
