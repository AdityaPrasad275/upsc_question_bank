### Question 28
**1] Satellites used for telecommunication relay are kept in a geostationary orbit. A satellite is said to be in such as orbit when: [2011]**

1. The orbit is geosynchronous.
2. The orbit is circular.
3. The orbit lies in the plane of the earth’s equator.
4. The orbit is at an altitude of 22,236.

Which the correct answer using the codes given below:

a) 1,2,and 3 only 
b) 1,3 and 4 only

c) 2 and 4 only 
d) 1,2,3, and 4

**Answer:**
Ans: 
a) 1,2,and 3 only

**Explanation:**
- A geostationary orbit is a specific type of orbit around the Earth where a satellite orbits the planet at the same rate as the Earth’s rotation. This means the satellite appears to remain stationary relative to a fixed point on the Earth’s surface. In other words, **a geostationary satellite appears to hover over a single location on the equator**, making it ideal for applications such as communication, weather monitoring, and navigation.

- The altitude of a geostationary orbit is approximately 35,786 kilometres (22,236 miles) above the Earth’s surface.

- A geostationary orbit is always in the plane of the equator i.e. directly above the Earth’s equator. This alignment ensures that the satellite appears to remain stationary relative to a fixed point on the Earth’s surface.

- As for its shape, a geostationary orbit is typically circular. This circular orbit is necessary to maintain a constant distance between the satellite and the Earth’s surface as it revolves around the planet. However, deviations from a perfect circle can occur due to various factors such as gravitational influences from other celestial bodies and perturbations from the Earth’s non-uniform mass distribution.

- Geosynchronous refers to an orbit where a satellite orbits the Earth in exactly 24 hours, matching the Earth’s rotation period. This type of orbit allows the satellite to remain fixed relative to the same point on the Earth’s surface over time, **though it may not necessarily be directly above the equator like in a geostationary orbit.** Geosynchronous orbits are used for various purposes, including communication, navigation, and Earth observation. **While all geostationary orbits are geosynchronous, not all geosynchronous orbits are geostationary.**

- **Thus, 1st, 2nd and 3rd statements are true, while the 4th is not.**
