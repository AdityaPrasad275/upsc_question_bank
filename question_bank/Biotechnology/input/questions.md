**Answer:**
Ans:

**Explanation:**
- RNA interference (RNAi) is a biological process in which RNA molecules inhibit gene expression or translation by neutralizing targeted mRNA molecules. This mechanism is used by cells to regulate gene activity and defend against viral infections. RNAi technology leverages this natural process for research and therapeutic purposes.

- Mechanism

- The process starts with the introduction of double-stranded RNA (dsRN
A) into the cell. The dsRNA is cleaved by an enzyme called Dicer into small interfering RNAs (siRNAs), typically 20-25 nucleotides long. The siRNAs are incorporated into the RNA-induced silencing complex (RIS
C).

- The siRNA within the RISC guides the complex to a complementary mRNA molecule. The RISC then cleaves and degrades the target mRNA, preventing it from being translated into a protein.

- **Applications**

- **RNAi can be used to silence specific genes to study their function by observing the effects of reduced gene expression.**

- **RNAi has potential in treating diseases by silencing disease-causing genes. This includes applications in cancer, viral infections, and genetic disorders.**

- **RNAi technology can also be used to develop crops with desirable traits, such as pest resistance or improved nutritional content.**
