### Question 27
**1] What are the reasons for the people’s resistance to the introduction of Bt brinjal in India? [2012]**

1. Bt brinjal has been created by inserting a gene from a soil fungus into its genome.
2. The seeds of Bt brinjal are terminator seeds and therefore, the farmers have to buy the seeds before every season from the seed companies.
3. There is an apprehension that the consumption of Bt brinjal may have adverse impact on health.
4. There is some concern that the introduction of Bt brinjal may have adverse effect on the biodiversity.

Select the correct answer using the codes given below:

a) 1, 2 &amp; 3 Only 
b) 2 &amp; 3 only

c) 3 &amp; 4 only 
d) 1,2,3 &amp; 4

**Answer:**
Ans: 
c) 3 &amp; 4 only

**Explanation:**
- Bt brinjal, also known as genetically modified (GM) brinjal or eggplant, is a variety of brinjal that has been genetically engineered to express a protein derived from the bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt).

- Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) is a naturally occurring soil bacterium commonly found in diverse environments, including soil, water, and decaying organic matter.

- This protein, known as Bt toxin, is toxic to certain insect pests that commonly attack brinjal crops, such as the fruit and shoot borer (FS
B).

- However, there is resistance to the introduction of Bt Brinjal in India, because of:

- **Environmental Concerns:** Critics raised concerns about the potential environmental impacts of Bt brinjal cultivation, including the risk of gene flow to non-GM crops, the development of insect resistance to Bt toxin, and unintended harm to non-target organisms such as beneficial insects.

- **Food Safety:** Some opponents expressed concerns about the safety of consuming genetically modified (GM) foods, citing potential health risks associated with Bt toxin ingestion and the lack of long-term studies on the health effects of Bt brinjal consumption.

- **Loss of Biodiversity:** Opponents argued that widespread cultivation of Bt brinjal could lead to the loss of traditional brinjal varieties and biodiversity, as farmers might shift exclusively to growing Bt brinjal hybrids, thereby reducing genetic diversity in brinjal crops.

- **Farmers’ Rights and Autonomy:** Critics raised questions about farmers’ rights and autonomy, expressing concerns that the introduction of Bt brinjal could lead to dependence on multinational seed companies, increased seed costs, and loss of control over seed saving and exchange practices. **(However, Bt Brinjal seeds are not terminator seeds)**

- **Lack of Transparency and Public Consultation:** Some stakeholders criticized the regulatory process for Bt brinjal approval, citing a lack of transparency, inadequate public consultation, and alleged conflicts of interest among regulators and biotechnology companies.

- **Ethical and Socioeconomic Concerns:** Opponents raised ethical and socioeconomic concerns related to the unequal distribution of benefits and risks associated with GM crops, potential impacts on traditional farming systems and livelihoods, and the need for broader societal debates on agricultural biotechnology.

- **Thus, statements 3 and 4 are correct.**
