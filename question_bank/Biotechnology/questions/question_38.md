**Answer:**
Ans: 
d) 3 and 4* (Answer have changed with time, refer explanation)

**Explanation:**
- Biotechnology is a rapidly changing field. It is difficult to tell what was the exact situation in 2002, we will answer this question on the basis of present (2024) technology and knowledge.

- Stem cells are unique cells found in the body with the remarkable ability to develop into many different cell types during early life and growth. They serve as the body’s natural repair system, dividing indefinitely to replenish other cells as long as the person or animal is alive. When a stem cell divides, each new cell has the potential to either remain a stem cell or become another type of cell with a more specialized function, such as a muscle cell, red blood cell, or brain cell.

- **Stem cells are present in various tissues and organs throughout the human body**, playing essential roles in tissue maintenance, repair, and regeneration.

- One common source of stem cells is embryonic stem cells (ESCs), which are derived from the inner cell mass of blastocysts, the early-stage embryos formed during the development of a fertilized egg.

- Another source of stem cells is adult stem cells, also known as somatic or tissue-specific stem cells. Examples of adult stem cells include hematopoietic stem cells found in bone marrow, neural stem cells found in the brain, and mesenchymal stem cells found in various connective tissues.

- **Recently, new techniques have been developed which will allow the extraction of stem cells without destroying the blastocyst. (This was not true in 2002, when the question was aske

- **Stem cells can regenerate themselves in vitro (in a laboratory setting) virtually forever,** under certain conditions. Stem cells possess the unique ability to undergo self-renewal, which means they can divide and produce identical copies of themselves indefinitely. This property allows stem cells to maintain their population and replenish the pool of undifferentiated stem cells over time.

- It is also true that Indian research centers have contributed to the creation of cell lines that have the potential to be developed into many types of tissues. Cell lines are populations of cells that have been cultured and propagated in a laboratory setting, often derived from primary tissues or organs. These cell lines serve as valuable tools for studying cellular processes, disease mechanisms, drug screening, and regenerative medicine applications.
