### Question
**1] With reference to the sectors of the Indian economy, consider the following pairs [2024]

**Economic activity – Sector
1. Storage of agricultural produce – Secondary
2. Dairy farm – Primary
3. Mineral exploration – Tertiary
4. Weaving cloth – Secondary

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched? 
a) Only one
b) Only two
c) Only three
d) All four

**Answer:**
Ans: b) Only two

**Explanation:**
- Primary sector involves the extraction and harvesting of natural resources. This sector is focused on raw materials that are directly taken from the earth e.g. growing crops (like rice or wheat), mining coal, extracting oil, fishing, logging etc.



- Secondary sector involves processing raw materials from the primary sector into finished goods or products. It is focused on manufacturing and construction e.g. producing cars, construction of buildings, refining oil into petrol, making textiles or clothes etc.



- Tertiary sector is focused on providing services rather than goods. It supports the functioning of the primary and secondary sectors by offering various services e.g. teachers, doctors, bank employees, software developers, taxi drivers, retail store workers etc.



- 1] Storage of agricultural produce falls under the tertiary sector since it is a service related to the handling and distribution of agricultural goods rather than manufacturing. **This makes first pair incorrect.**



- 2] A dairy farm is part of the primary sector because it involves the production of raw agricultural products (milk) directly from animals. **Thus, second pair is correct**.



- 3] Mineral exploration is about finding and evaluating mineral deposits. Although it may appear service sector, according to National Industrial Classification 2008, Mining exploration pertains to primary sector. **Thus, third pair is incorrect.**



- Weaving cloth is a manufacturing process, placing it in the secondary sector as it transforms raw materials (like cotton or silk) into finished goods, **making fourth pair, correct.**



- Thus, b) Only two, is the correct answer.

### Question
**4] Despite being a high saving economy, capital formation may not result in significant increase in output due to: [2018]

**a) Weak administrative machinery
b) Illiteracy
c) High population density
d) High capital-output ratio

**Answer:**
Ans: d) High capital-output ratio

**Explanation:**
- **Capital formation**&nbsp;refers to the process of saving and investing money to create new capital goods (machinery, equipment, infrastructure).



- A high saving rate in an economy is generally considered positive as it provides funds for investment.




However, simply having a high saving rate doesn’t guarantee a significant increase in output (production). The impact of capital formation on output depends on the **capital-output ratio (COR)**.






- **Capital-output ratio** measures the amount of additional capital investment required to produce one unit of additional output.



A **high capital-output ratio** indicates that a large amount of capital investment is needed to generate a relatively small increase in output. This can happen due to various reasons:

- **Inefficient industries:**&nbsp;Existing industries might be outdated or inefficient, requiring significant investments just to maintain current output levels, not necessarily increase them.



- **Infrastructure bottlenecks:**&nbsp;Lack of proper infrastructure like transportation, power, or communication networks can hinder the utilization of new capital effectively.



- **Technological limitations:**&nbsp;If the economy lacks access to advanced technologies, even large investments might not translate into substantial output growth.

### Question
**2] The national income of a country for a given period is equal to the __? [2013]

**a) Total value of goods and services produced by the nationals
b) Sum of total consumption and investment expenditure
c) Sum of personal income of all individuals
d) Money value of final goods and services produced

**Answer:**
Ans: d) Money value of final goods and services produced

**Explanation:**
**National Income: A Measure of Economic Output**





National income represents the total market value of all **final goods and services** produced in an economy within a specific period (usually a year). It’s a crucial metric for gauging a country’s economic performance and growth.





**National Income vs. Other Economic Concepts:**





It’s important to distinguish national income from other related concepts:






- **Gross Domestic Product (GDP):**&nbsp;This is very similar to national income. In most cases, they are identical. However, GDP considers the total value of production within a country’s geographical borders, regardless of the nationality of the producers. National income, on the other hand, focuses on production by a country’s nationals, even if it occurs abroad. This difference is usually negligible for most countries.



- **Gross National Income (GNI):**&nbsp;This is another closely related concept. It’s equal to national income plus net income from abroad (income earned by a country’s residents from foreign investments minus income paid to foreign residents for their investments in the country).



- **Personal Income:**&nbsp;This refers to the income received by individuals before taxes and other deductions. It’s a smaller measure than national income, as it doesn’t include business profits or undistributed corporate income.
