### Question 6
**1] Consider the following: [2021]
**
1) Bacteria
2) Fungi
3) Virus

Which of the above can be cultured in artificial/synthetic medium?

a) 1 and 2 only

b) 2 and 3 only

c) 1 and 3 only

d) 1, 2 and 3

**Answer:**
Ans: 
a) 1 and 2 only

**Explanation:**
Bacteria and fungi can be cultured in artificial/synthetic medium, whereas viruses cannot be cultured in such conditions.

**Explanation**: Artificial/synthetic medium refers to a laboratory-made nutrient-rich environment that provides all the necessary components for the growth and reproduction of microorganisms. It typically consists of various chemicals, salts, vitamins, and energy sources.

**1. Bacteria: **Bacteria are single-celled microorganisms that can be cultured in artificial/synthetic medium. They can utilize the nutrients present in the medium to grow and reproduce. Bacterial culture is a common technique used in microbiology laboratories to study their morphology, physiology, and metabolism, as well as to perform various diagnostic tests.

**2. Fungi:** Fungi are eukaryotic microorganisms that can also be cultured in artificial/synthetic medium. Similar to bacteria, fungi require a nutrient-rich environment to grow and reproduce. Fungal culture is widely used in laboratories to identify and characterize different fungal species, study their growth patterns, and perform antifungal susceptibility testing.

**3. Virus:** Viruses, on the other hand, cannot be cultured in artificial/synthetic medium alone. Viruses are not considered living organisms because they lack the cellular machinery required for independent growth and reproduction. Instead, viruses require a host cell to replicate and complete their life cycle. They infect host cells, hijack their cellular machinery, and utilize their resources to produce new viral particles.

In order to study viruses in the laboratory, scientists use host cells that are susceptible to viral infection. These host cells can be cultured in artificial/synthetic medium, providing the necessary nutrients for both the host cells and the viruses. This allows the viruses to infect the host cells and replicate within them. The resulting viral particles can then be harvested and studied further.

In conclusion, bacteria and fungi can be cultured in artificial/synthetic medium, while viruses require a host cell and cannot be cultured in such conditions alone.
