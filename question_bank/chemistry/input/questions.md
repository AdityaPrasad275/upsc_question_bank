### Question 5
**2] Water can dissolve more substances than any other liquid because: [2021]
**

a) It is dipolar in nature

b) It is a good conductor of heat

c) It has high value of specific heat

d) It is an oxide of hydrogen

**Answer:**
Ans: 
a) It is dipolar in nature

**Explanation:**
Water’s remarkable ability to dissolve a wide variety of substances stems from its unique molecular structure and polarity. A water molecule consists of two hydrogen atoms bonded to one oxygen atom, forming a bent shape. The oxygen atom, being more electronegative, exerts a stronger pull on the shared electrons, creating a partial negative charge (δ-) on the oxygen and partial positive charges (δ+) on the hydrogen atoms. This polarity gives water a dipole moment, allowing it to interact with other polar molecules or ions through electrostatic attractions.

Furthermore, water molecules can form hydrogen bonds with each other and with other molecules. These hydrogen bonds enable water to effectively surround and solvate solute particles, breaking them apart and dispersing them throughout the solvent.

This combination of polarity and hydrogen bonding makes water an exceptionally effective solvent, capable of dissolving a vast range of substances, including salts, sugars, acids, and gases. This property is fundamental to many biological processes, as water serves as the medium in which biochemical reactions occur within living organisms. Thus, water’s role as the “universal solvent” is essential for life as we know it.

### Question 6
**1] In the context of which one of the following are the terms ‘pyrolysis and plasma gasification’ mentioned? [2019]
**

a) Extraction of rare earth elements

b) Natural gas extraction technologies

c) Hydrogen fuel-based automobiles

d) Waste-to-energy technologies

**Answer:**
Ans: 
d) Waste-to-energy technologies

**Explanation:**
Pyrolysis and plasma gasification are both waste-to-energy technologies aimed at converting waste materials into usable energy sources while minimizing environmental impact.

Pyrolysis involves the thermal decomposition of organic materials in the absence of oxygen. Under high temperatures, waste materials such as biomass, plastics, and organic waste are heated, causing them to break down into gases, liquids, and char. These byproducts can then be used to generate heat, electricity, biofuels, or chemicals.

Plasma gasification, on the other hand, utilizes an ionized gas known as plasma to break down waste materials into their constituent elements at extremely high temperatures. In this process, waste is subjected to a plasma torch, which generates temperatures exceeding 3,000°C, causing waste to dissociate into syngas (a mixture of hydrogen and carbon monoxide) and slag. The syngas can be further processed to produce electricity, fuels, or chemicals, while the slag can be utilized in construction materials or other applications.

Both pyrolysis and plasma gasification offer several advantages over traditional waste disposal methods, including reduced greenhouse gas emissions, diversion of waste from landfills, and production of valuable energy or chemical products. These technologies play a crucial role in sustainable waste management practices, contributing to the circular economy by converting waste into valuable resources.

**1] Which of the following is/are the example/examples of chemical change? [2014]
**
1. Crystallization of sodium chloride
2. Melting of ice
3. Souring of milk

Select the correct answer using the code given below.

a) 1 and 2 only

b) 3 only

c) 1, 2 and 3

d) None

**Answer:**
Ans: 
b) 3 only

**Explanation:**
Out of the given options, only option 3, i.e., souring of milk is an example of chemical change. Chemical changes are those changes in which one or more substances are transformed into a new substance with different physical and chemical properties. In the case of souring of milk, the lactic acid bacteria present in the milk metabolize the lactose sugar in the milk, which leads to the formation of lactic acid. This change is irreversible and results in a new substance with different properties than milk.

On the other hand, crystallization of sodium chloride and melting of ice are examples of physical changes. In these changes, there is no formation of new substances with different chemical properties. In the case of crystallization of sodium chloride, the solid sodium chloride is simply separated from the solution, and in the case of melting of ice, the solid ice is converted into liquid water without any change in the chemical composition of the substance.

### Question 10
**1] Fruits stored in cold chamber exhibit long storage life because ___: [2013]
**

a) Exposure to sunlight is prevented

b) Concentration of Carbon dioxide in the environment is increased

c) Rate of respiration is decreased

d) There is an increase in humidity

**Answer:**
Ans: 
c) Rate of respiration is decreased

**Explanation:**
Storing fruits in a cold chamber extends their storage life primarily by reducing the rate of respiration. Respiration is a vital metabolic process in fruits, wherein they consume oxygen and release carbon dioxide, water, and energy. Lowering the temperature slows down biochemical reactions, including respiration, which significantly reduces the fruits’ metabolic activity. As a result, the fruits undergo less physiological changes associated with ripening, such as softening, color changes, and flavor development.

Cold temperatures also inhibit the growth of microorganisms that can cause spoilage, further preserving the fruits’ quality. Additionally, cold storage helps to minimize water loss from fruits, preventing dehydration and maintaining their firmness and juiciness.

Moreover, storing fruits in a cold chamber creates a controlled environment where exposure to sunlight and fluctuating temperatures is minimized. This controlled environment helps to maintain optimal conditions for fruit storage, ensuring that the fruits remain fresh for a longer period.

In essence, by slowing down respiration, inhibiting microbial growth, reducing water loss, and providing a controlled environment, cold storage significantly extends the storage life of fruits, allowing them to be stored for extended periods without compromising their quality or freshness.

**1] Aspartame is an artificial sweetener sold in the market. It consist of amino acids and provides calories like other amino acids. Yet, it is used as a low-calorie sweetening agent in food items. What is the basis of this use? [2011]**


a) Aspartame is as sweet as table sugar, but unlike table sugar, it is not readily oxidized in human body due to lack of requisite enzymes.

b) When aspartame is used in food processing, the sweet taste remains, but it becomes resistant to oxidation.

c) Aspartame is as sweet as sugar, but after ingestion into the body, it is converted into metabolites that yield no
calories.

d) Aspartame is several times sweeter than table sugar, hence food items made with small quantities of aspartame yield fewer calories on oxidation.

**Answer:**
Ans: 
d) Aspartame is several times sweeter than table sugar, hence food items made with small quantities of aspartame yield fewer calories on oxidation.

**Explanation:**
Aspartame, an artificial sweetener, is utilized as a low-calorie sweetening agent in food items due to its intense sweetness compared to table sugar (sucrose). Aspartame is several times sweeter than sucrose, meaning only a small amount is needed to achieve the desired level of sweetness in foods and beverages. This characteristic allows food manufacturers to reduce the overall calorie content of their products while still providing a satisfying sweetness.

Moreover, unlike sugar, which contributes four calories per gram, aspartame itself provides very few calories. When ingested, aspartame is broken down into its component parts: amino acids (the building blocks of proteins) and methanol. These breakdown products do not contribute significantly to calorie intake as the body either metabolizes them for energy or eliminates them. As a result, foods sweetened with aspartame contain fewer calories overall, making them suitable for individuals seeking to manage their calorie intake or control their weight without sacrificing taste.

In essence, the combination of aspartame’s intense sweetness and its minimal calorie contribution makes it an attractive option for food manufacturers and consumers alike, enabling the creation and consumption of low-calorie or sugar-free food and beverage products.
