### Question 11
**1] What is the role of ultraviolet (UV) radiation in the water purification systems? [2012]**

1. It inactivates/kills the harmful microorganisms in water.
2. It removes all the undesirable odours from the water.
3. It quickens the sedimentation of solid particles, removes turbidity and improves the clarity of water.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

a) 1 Only 
b) 2 &amp; 3 Only

c) 1 &amp; 3 Only 
d) 1,2 &amp; 3

**Answer:**
Ans: 
a) 1 Only

**Explanation:**
- **It inactivates/kills the harmful microorganisms in water:** This statement is correct. Ultraviolet (UV) radiation is an effective method for disinfecting water by inactivating a wide range of harmful microorganisms, including bacteria, viruses, and protozoa. UV light damages the genetic material of these microorganisms, preventing them from reproducing and causing illness. Thus, UV radiation plays a crucial role in ensuring the safety of drinking water by eliminating pathogens.

- **It removes all the undesirable odors from the water:** This statement is incorrect. UV radiation is not effective in removing odors from water. Odors in water are typically caused by dissolved organic compounds, such as hydrogen sulfide or organic matter, which are not affected by UV treatment. Other purification methods, such as activated carbon filtration or chemical treatments, are more suitable for removing odors from water.

- **It quickens the sedimentation of solid particles, removes turbidity, and improves the clarity of water:** This statement is also incorrect. UV radiation does not affect the sedimentation of solid particles or the turbidity of water. UV treatment specifically targets microorganisms, and it does not have a direct impact on the physical properties of water such as turbidity or sedimentation. These aspects are typically addressed through processes like sedimentation, filtration, or coagulation.
