### Question 15
**8] Which one of the following genetic diseases is sex-linked? [1999]**


a) Royal haemophilia

b) Tay-Sachs disease

c) Cystic fibrosis

d) Hypertension

**Answer:**
Ans: 
a) Royal haemophilia

**Explanation:**
Royal hemophilia, also known as hemophilia B, is a genetic disease that is sex-linked, meaning it is carried on the X chromosome (option 
a). This disorder primarily affects males, as they have only one X chromosome, inherited from their mother. If a male inherits a defective X chromosome containing the gene for hemophilia from his mother, he will have the disorder because he lacks a second X chromosome to offset the defective gene. Females, on the other hand, have two X chromosomes, so if one carries the defective gene, the other X chromosome may carry a healthy version, reducing the severity of the disorder or providing protection altogether.

Hemophilia is characterized by impaired blood clotting, leading to prolonged bleeding episodes even from minor injuries. Affected individuals may experience spontaneous bleeding into joints and muscles, which can cause pain, swelling, and long-term joint damage if left untreated. Treatment typically involves replacement of the deficient clotting factor through intravenous infusions of clotting factor concentrates to control bleeding episodes and prevent complications.
