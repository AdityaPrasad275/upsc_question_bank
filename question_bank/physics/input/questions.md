**Explanation:**
**Dispersion: **– Dispersion is the phenomenon of splitting of white light into its constituent colors due to the variation of refractive index with wavelength.
– When the sunlight falls on a raindrop, it gets refracted and dispersed into its constituent colors.
– The different colors of light have different wavelengths, so they bend at different angles and hence get separated.

**Refraction:** – Refraction is the bending of light when it passes from one medium to another of different refractive indices.
– When the sunlight enters a raindrop, it gets refracted at the point where the air and water meet.
– The amount of bending of light depends on the angle of incidence and the refractive indices of the two media.

**Internal Reflection:** – Internal reflection is the reflection of light within a medium when it strikes the boundary of another medium at an angle greater than the critical angle.
– When the refracted light inside the raindrop hits the inner surface of the drop, it undergoes internal reflection.
– The angle of incidence at which the internal reflection occurs is different for different colors of light.

**Combination of Phenomena:** – The combination of these three phenomena results in the formation of a rainbow.
– When the dispersed light undergoes internal reflection inside the raindrop, it forms a cone-shaped region of colors.
– These colors emerge from the raindrop and form a circular arc in the sky with red color on the outer side and violet on the inner side.

### Question 17
**1] The function of heavy water in a nuclear reactor is to? [2011]**


a) Slow down the speed of neutrons.

b) Increase the speed of neutrons.

c) Cool down the reactor.

d) Stop the nuclear reaction.

**Answer:**
Ans: 
a) Slow down the speed of neutrons.

**Explanation:**
Heavy water, also known as deuterium oxide (D2O), serves a crucial role in certain types of nuclear reactors, particularly pressurized heavy water reactors (PHWRs). Its primary function is to moderate or slow down the speed of neutrons produced during nuclear fission reactions.

In nuclear reactors, neutrons released during fission reactions are initially fast-moving. However, fast neutrons are less efficient at causing further fission events. Heavy water acts as an efficient neutron moderator because its deuterium nuclei have a high neutron scattering cross-section. As neutrons collide with the hydrogen nuclei in heavy water, they lose kinetic energy, resulting in a moderated neutron with reduced speed.

Slowing down neutrons increases the probability of their interaction with fissile nuclei, such as uranium-235 or plutonium-239, and initiates further fission reactions. This moderation process helps sustain the nuclear chain reaction within the reactor core. Therefore, heavy water facilitates the efficient and controlled generation of energy in nuclear reactors by regulating the speed of neutrons involved in the fission process.

### Question 20
**4] The surface of a lake is frozen in severe winter, but the water at its bottom is still liquid. What is the reason? [2011]**


a) Ice is a bad conductor of heat.

b) Since the surface of the lake is at the same temperature as the air, no heat is lost.

c) The density of water is maximum at 4°c.

d) None of the statements (
a), (
b) and (
c) given is correct.

**Answer:**
Ans: 
c) The density of water is maximum at 4°c.

**Explanation:**
The unique behavior of water, specifically its density anomaly, is the primary reason why the surface of a lake freezes in severe winter while the water at its bottom remains liquid. Water exhibits an unusual density pattern as it cools: it reaches its maximum density at approximately 4°C. As the temperature continues to drop below 4°C, the density of water decreases, causing it to expand rather than contract like most substances.

In a lake, when the surface temperature drops below the freezing point (0°
C), the colder water at the surface becomes less dense and forms ice, which floats on top of the liquid water. Meanwhile, the relatively warmer water at the bottom of the lake remains liquid because it is insulated by the layer of ice above. This insulation prevents further heat loss from the liquid water below, allowing it to remain in a liquid state despite the freezing conditions above.

This phenomenon of density-driven stratification ensures that aquatic life can survive even in extremely cold environments, as the liquid water at the bottom of the lake provides a refuge from the freezing temperatures above.
